# Social Studies question-bank overview (Knowledge Showdown 1.3)

**Please ask the Social Studies department to review the question sets for local sequencing and accuracy before instructional assessment use.** This is a practice game and does not certify Washington-standard alignment. Some topics, especially tribal history and treaty interpretation, should be reviewed against current tribally developed and OSPI-approved educational resources.

## Selectable boards

Every Social Studies grade has eight topic choices. Teachers select exactly **six** to fill the game board's six columns. The five rows are 100, 200, 300, 400 and 500 points.

| Grade | General focus | Topics |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Early American history | First Peoples and Homelands; Exploration and Encounters; The 13 Colonies; Colonial Life; The American Revolution; Founding Documents; Early U.S. Government; Historical Sources |
| 6 | Ancient civilizations | Geography and Maps; Mesopotamia; Ancient Egypt; Ancient Greece; Ancient Rome; Ancient China; Trade and Technology; Compare Civilizations |
| 7 | Washington state history | Washington Geography; Yakama and Tribal Nations; Treaties and Sovereignty; Washington Territory; Statehood and Government; Natural Resources and Economy; Migration and Communities; Washington History Sources |
| 8 | U.S. Constitution and 19th-century history | The Constitution; Bill of Rights; Three Branches; Federalism and Elections; Westward Expansion; Slavery and Abolition; The Civil War; Reconstruction |

## Classroom notes

- Questions emphasize vocabulary, chronology, multiple perspectives, evidence use, and government basics.
- A subtopic currently has five authored questions—one at each point value. **The same subtopic and point value will repeat in subsequent games** until more variants are added.
- The original 510 mixed-board questions remain available under *Mixed Subjects*.
- Each clue includes a correct response, distractors, and a short explanation. Optional fifth and sixth answer choices are drawn from the related question pool; teachers should review those extra distractors.
- Saved matches are in browser storage; switching topics starts a fresh match, but existing question memory is retained where browser storage is available.
- Teacher review is particularly important for cultural and historical interpretation; this is not a replacement for primary sources or local instructional materials.
